// Stat Definitions — from The Sports Statistics Manual by Chris Cain
OVR MLB
Overall Rating
A 60–99 composite score built on the same power curve as the NFL OVR. For pitchers, based on RPI normalized against the season's best and worst. For batters, based on weighted offensive production relative to league average. 99 = best in the league, 60 = at league minimum.
OVR = 60 + 39 × ((value − MIN) / (MAX − MIN))1.35
RPI MLB
Runs Per Inning
The primary pitching efficiency metric in this system. Measures earned runs allowed per inning pitched on a per-inning scale rather than per-9-inning scale like ERA. Lower is better. Treats partial innings correctly — a pitcher who allows 2 runs in 6.1 innings is scored more precisely than ERA's rounding would suggest. The foundation of the pitcher OVR calculation.
RPI = ER ÷ IP (with innings expressed as decimals: 6.1 IP = 6.333)
Skubal 2025: 48 ER ÷ 195.1 IP = RPI 0.246 — elite. Gallen: 103 ÷ 192 = 0.536 — well below average despite pitching the same number of innings.
RPG MLB
Runs Per Game
A batter production metric measuring offensive run contribution per game played. Accounts for both runs scored and runs driven in, giving a complete picture of a hitter's contribution to the scoreboard. Team-dependent by nature — a great hitter on a weak lineup will have a suppressed RPG because fewer runners are on base and fewer teammates can drive him in.
RPG = (R + RBI) ÷ G
RpPA MLB
Runs per Plate Appearance
A proposed batter efficiency metric measuring direct run production per trip to the plate. Subtracts home runs once to avoid double-counting solo HRs that appear in both R and RBI. More granular than RPG because it accounts for how often a player actually bats. Like RPG, it is team-context dependent — Trout's years with weak Angels lineups are a clear example of how lineup quality suppresses this number for great hitters.
RpPA = (R + RBI − HR) ÷ PA
Ohtani 2025: (115R + 130RBI − 44HR) ÷ 622PA = 0.324 — a strong RpPA reflecting his dual role as scorer and run-producer.
ERA MLB
Earned Run Average
The traditional pitching benchmark — earned runs allowed per 9 innings. Still the most widely cited pitching stat. RPI refines ERA for partial-inning precision; FIP and xFIP strip out defense to isolate what the pitcher controls.
ERA = (ER × 9) ÷ IP
ERA below 3.00 is elite; 3.00–4.00 is above average; above 5.00 is poor in most eras.
FIP MLB
Fielding Independent Pitching
An ERA-equivalent using only outcomes the pitcher fully controls: strikeouts, walks, hit-by-pitches, and home runs. Removes fielding quality from the equation. FIP that is significantly lower than ERA suggests the pitcher was unlucky or played behind a poor defense.
FIP = (13×HR + 3×(BB+HBP) − 2×K) ÷ IP + FIP constant
WHIP MLB
Walks + Hits Per Inning Pitched
Baserunners allowed per inning. Below 1.00 is elite — fewer than one baserunner per inning. Above 1.40 is poor. Does not distinguish between how runners scored, making it a traffic measure rather than a damage measure.
WHIP = (BB + H) ÷ IP
xFIP MLB
Expected FIP
Like FIP but replaces actual home runs with expected home runs based on the pitcher's fly ball rate and the league-average HR/FB ratio. Stabilizes faster than FIP and is better for predicting future ERA, since HR/FB fluctuates more than underlying contact quality.
xFIP = (13×(FB×lgHR/FB) + 3×(BB+HBP) − 2×K) ÷ IP + FIP constant
OPS MLB
On-Base Plus Slugging
The most predictive single batting stat for run production — on-base percentage plus slugging percentage. League average is typically around .720. Above .900 is elite; below .650 is poor. Note that OBP and SLG are on different scales, making the addition technically imprecise — wOBA and wRC+ correct for this but are less intuitive.
OPS = OBP + SLG
WAR All
Wins Above Replacement
Combines batting, baserunning, and fielding (position players) or pitching (pitchers) into a single value expressing wins contributed above a replacement-level player. 5+ WAR is All-Star caliber; 8+ is MVP territory; negative WAR means the player hurt the team.
WAR = batting runs + baserunning runs + fielding runs + positional adjustment + league adjustment + replacement runs ÷ RPW
