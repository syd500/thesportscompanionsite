// Stat Definitions — from The Sports Statistics Manual by Chris Cain
OVR NFL
Overall Rating
A 60–99 composite score comparing a player or team to all others in the same category. Built on a power curve so elite outliers are rewarded more than a straight linear scale. 99 = best in the dataset, 60 = at or below league minimum.
OVR = 60 + 39 × ((value − MIN) / (MAX − MIN))1.35
The 1.35 exponent compresses the middle and stretches the top — a player at the 90th percentile scores higher than a linear model would give them.
PPD NFL
Points Per Down
A context-weighted efficiency metric calculated from play-by-play data. Every passing, rushing, or receiving play earns or loses points based on field position (five zones from own 1–19 to opponent 80–99), down (1st/2nd, 3rd, 4th), distance to go, and result (completion, incompletion, interception, fumble, rush gain bucket). Field position doubles in value each zone — a completion at the opponent 15 is worth ~16× the same completion at your own 15. Calculated separately per position: QBs use passing plays, RBs use rushing plays, WRs/TEs use receiving plays. Within-position comparison only — a QB's PPD cannot be compared to a RB's PPD.
PPD = Σ(play point values) ÷ total plays evaluated
Mahomes 2024: PPD = 2.84 — every down he was involved in produced an average of 2.84 context-weighted points toward a scoring drive.
PPR NFL
Points Per Reception
A receiver efficiency metric using the same contextual point values as PPD — each reception is scored by field zone, down, and distance to go. Rewards receivers who make catches in high-leverage situations (3rd and short in the red zone) over those who pad stats on low-value catches. Calculated from receiving plays only. Note: PPR also refers to a common fantasy football scoring format — context will always clarify which is meant.
PPR = Σ(reception point values) ÷ total receiving plays
A WR catching a 9-yard pass on 3rd & 8 at the opponent 22 scores 0.660 points — far more than the same catch on 1st & 10 at his own 25 (0.158).
PPK NFL
Points Per Kick
A kicker efficiency metric that weights field goals by distance and includes extra points. Longer kicks earn more credit — a 55-yarder is worth meaningfully more than a 28-yarder, reflecting the difficulty difference. A pure FG% ignores this; PPK rewards both accuracy and range.
PPK = Σ(FGM × distance weight + XPM) ÷ total kick attempts
Tucker career PPK ranks highest all-time because he combines elite accuracy with willingness to attempt — and make — kicks most kickers decline.
PPP NFL
Points Per Punt
A punter efficiency metric combining net yards, touchbacks (negative — give the offense a free 25 yards), and punts downed inside the 20 (positive — pins opponents deep). Net average alone misses field position context; PPP captures the full picture of what a punt actually accomplished.
PPP = f(NetYds, TB, Pnt20) ÷ total punts
A punter who averages 44 net yards but has 8 touchbacks scores lower than one averaging 41 net yards with 0 touchbacks and 18 punts inside the 20.
PPY NFL
Points Per Yard
A team-level metric measuring how efficiently offensive yardage converts to actual points. Two teams can gain the same yards per game but score very differently based on red zone efficiency, turnover rate, and situational execution. PPY captures that gap.
PPY = Points Scored ÷ Total Offensive Yards
A team converting at 0.065 PPY is elite — roughly 1 point every 15 yards. Below 0.050 suggests red zone or turnover issues despite solid yardage numbers.
YPPG NFL
Yards Per Play — Net
A net offensive efficiency metric combining yards gained, yards allowed, turnovers created, and turnovers committed per game. Weights turnover differential heavily (×3 per turnover) because possession changes drive wins more than raw yardage. The strongest single predictor of team success in the dataset.
YPPG = ((Off Yds × 0.08) + (Opp TO × 3) − (Def Yds × 0.08) − (Own TO × 3)) ÷ G
Rams 2025: YPPG = 7.31 — best in the league, combining elite offense, low own turnovers, and a defense that forced turnovers at a high rate.
OYPPG NFL
Opponent YPPG
Defensive equivalent of YPPG — yards per play allowed by the defense. Lower is better. Used alongside YPPG to compute the differential that predicts wins.
OYPPG = Defensive yards allowed per play (adjusted)
YPPG Diff NFL
YPPG Differential
Offensive YPPG minus Opponent YPPG. A positive number means the team creates more net value per play than it surrenders. The single metric most correlated with winning percentage in this system.
YPPG Diff = YPPG − OYPPG
A team with YPPG Diff of +1.5 wins roughly 70%+ of games. Below 0 means the defense is consistently outplayed by the offenses it faces.
RTG NFL
Passer Rating
The NFL's official QB rating combining completion %, yards per attempt, TD%, and INT% on a 0–158.3 scale. 100+ is elite, 85–99 is above average, below 70 is poor. A baseline benchmark — PPD provides the contextual layer RTG lacks.
RTG = (a + b + c + d) × 100 ÷ 6 where each component is capped 0–2.375
WAR All
Wins Above Replacement
How many wins a player contributes above a league-average replacement. Imported from baseball analytics — in football it combines offensive, defensive, and special teams value into a single win total.
WAR = position-adjusted production above replacement level
